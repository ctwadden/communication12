02 · Step One

Choose a client, then make a campaign decision.

Each client has products, services, audience clues, and simple data. Your job is to decide what the client should promote and explain why.

Agency thinking: Do not just pick the easiest item to design. Look at the tables and graphs. Decide whether the client should promote a strong seller, fix a weak area, reach a new audience, or advertise a service people do not know about.

05 · Moodboard

Build a simple moodboard before production.

Your moodboard is your visual plan. It helps you decide colours, fonts, photo style, layout, and the feeling of your campaign before you start building final pieces.

Option 1: Use Mr. Wadden's Moodboard Template

This is the easiest option if you want a simple online page made for this project. Open the template, follow the prompts, and use it to organize your campaign ideas.

Open the Communication 12 Moodboard Template

Option 2: Use Canva

Canva is a free online option with ready-made moodboard templates. Use it if you prefer drag-and-drop images, colours, fonts, and notes.

Open Canva Mood Board Maker

Quick Tutorial: Creating a Moodboard

Watch this short tutorial before building your moodboard. Focus on how the examples use colour, images, fonts, and visual style to guide a design direction.

Open the Moodboard Tutorial on YouTube

Your moodboard must include: 6–10 visual references, 3–5 colour swatches, 2 font/style ideas, 2 layout ideas, 3 words describing the brand mood, and one sentence explaining how the moodboard fits your client and audience.

07 · Process Evidence

Daily logs show that the project was built over time.

Students will complete the separate daily log sheet you provide. This section tells them what evidence matters.

Daily log should prove:

  • What I worked on today
  • Which client/product this supports
  • Which module/outcome this connects to
  • Screenshot, photo, file, or link showing progress
  • What problem I solved or what I need next

Process evidence can include:

  • Moodboard link
  • Sketches or thumbnails
  • Shot list or storyboard
  • Photos/contact sheet
  • Photoshop layer screenshot
  • Video timeline screenshot
  • Website draft screenshot
  • Peer feedback and revision notes
No evidence = weak process mark. A finished-looking final product without logs, screenshots, drafts, or revisions does not fully demonstrate the process outcomes.
08 · Bad Example

What a developing submission looks like.

Keep this example. It helps students see why “I made a poster and a website” may still only be a developing project.

Sample: SoundNest

  • It is loud
  • It is portable

Why this is mediocre

1

Good image, weak process

The image looks polished, but the student did not show how it was created, selected, edited, or used as part of a larger campaign. There is no contact sheet, setup photo, or editing/process evidence.

2

Too few design decisions

Mostly title, image, and slogan. The product image looks good, but the layout, typography, spacing, and branding system are still weak.

3

Unclear third format

Poster and website are present, but the third build format/module is not meaningfully demonstrated.

4

Weak website

The website is a heading and three bullets, not a product landing page.

5

No process evidence

No moodboard, screenshots, sketches, feedback, or revision notes.

6

Basic reflection

The reflection says what was made but does not explain design choices or outcome connections.
